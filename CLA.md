## What this asks of you, in plain words

WinStoreRegion is released under **GPL-3.0-or-later**, and your contribution is
accepted under that licence and no other. This agreement does not ask you to
allow the project to be relicensed later, and it does not ask you to hand over
your copyright. You keep it, and you remain free to use your own work however
you like elsewhere.

What it does ask is narrow and, for most people, uncontroversial:

- that the work is yours to give;
- that you licence it under GPL-3.0-or-later, the same licence the project
  already uses;
- that any patents you hold which the work needs are licensed along with it, so
  the project cannot be sued for using what you contributed.

There is deliberately **no relicensing clause**. The project stays copyleft, and
nobody — maintainer or contributor — can quietly move it to other terms. The
practical consequence is worth knowing: changing the project's licence later
would require the agreement of every contributor individually, which in effect
means it will not happen. That is the intended outcome.

## How to agree

State the following in the description of your first pull request:

> I have read `CLA.md` and I agree to it for this and all my future
> contributions to this project.

One statement covers your later contributions as well; you do not need to repeat
it in every pull request.

If you are contributing on behalf of a company, say so, and confirm that the
person agreeing is authorised to bind it.

## A lighter alternative, if you prefer it

Because this agreement grants no relicensing right, it is close in effect to a
**Developer Certificate of Origin**
([developercertificate.org](https://developercertificate.org/)), where a
contributor certifies that they have the right to submit the work and signs off
each commit with `git commit -s`. The differences that remain are that this
document states the patent licence and the absence of warranty explicitly rather
than leaving them to GPL-3.0-or-later alone, and that agreement is recorded once
in words rather than in every commit trailer. If a DCO sign-off suits your
workflow better, say so in your pull request.
